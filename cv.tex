I am particularly interested in using category theory to extend algebraic and logical theories from classical equational settings to ordered and quantitative settings, and in exploring their applications in computer science.

\section*{Doctoral Thesis}
\textbf{Quantale-Enriched Algebras}\par
This thesis develops a unified framework for equational, ordered, and quantitative algebra using categories enriched over a commutative quantale. Its main results include a congruence--quotient correspondence, Birkhoff-style and Pavelka-style completeness for graded inequational logic, and enriched SP and HSP theorems characterizing classes of algebras definable by implications and inequations. These results also extend to separated algebras through separated reflection.

\section*{Formalization}
\textbf{\href{https://github.com/YiwenDing-Logic/modal-characterization-in-lean4}{Modal Characterization in Lean 4}}\hfill 2026\par
This project formalizes central results in classical modal model theory in Lean 4, building on mathlib and CSLib. It covers van Benthem's characterization theorem for basic unimodal logic, modal definability of pointed model classes, bisimulation-somewhere characterizations, and Hennessy--Milner theorems. Supporting constructions include saturation and ultraproducts.
